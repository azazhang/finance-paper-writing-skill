# Evidence and Claims in Finance Manuscripts

A finance manuscript's claims must be warranted by its current research evidence. The writing agent verifies the material facts **in the manuscript itself**, using the existing tables, figures, research outputs, and primary literature as needed.

## Inferential language

- **Descriptive:** documents a distribution, co-movement, or institutional fact.
- **Associational:** is associated with an outcome conditional on the reported controls.
- **Predictive:** predicts an outcome observed later, given the research design.
- **Mechanism-consistent:** supports an interpretation without isolating that channel.
- **Quasi-experimental or causal:** use causal verbs only when the actual identification strategy supports the statement, with relevant assumptions and limitations explained.

An evidence-confrontable scientific contribution need not always be a causal estimator. Use the strongest accurate claim with a clearly bounded scope.

## Translate estimates into economic evidence

For each headline quantitative statement, check against the relevant exhibit: observation and sample, outcome, explanatory-variable scale, benchmark or reference group, sign, estimate, units, denominator, time horizon, uncertainty, and named specification. Distinguish percentage changes from percentage points, annual from monthly quantities, and associations from identified effects. Give the finance reader an economically interpretable magnitude and explain the mechanism or competing interpretation.

Check literature attributions against the actual neighboring paper. Preserve source uncertainty; a citation does not make an inaccurate claim valid. Verify the affected claims rather than restarting the entire empirical analysis.

## Choose evidence for its intellectual job

Give main-text space to findings readers need to understand and assess the central economic answer: the decisive result, identifying comparison, meaningful magnitude, mechanism differentiation, and material adverse evidence. Place secondary diagnostics and detailed implementation checks in the appendix when they genuinely help assess the reported inference. Keep exploratory and superseded results in the research record.

A credible, sufficiently precise null can inform an economic prediction. An imprecise coefficient is evidence of uncertainty and must not be sold as proof of no effect. Preserve adverse findings that narrow the headline conclusion. If the evidence no longer supports the central claim, revise the claim.

For a full-paper revision, reconcile the title, abstract, introduction, results, tables and figures, and conclusion against the **same current evidence**. For a small revision, check the affected claim and its immediate context. Repair discrepancies directly in the manuscript.
