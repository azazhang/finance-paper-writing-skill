# Closest-Paper Positioning

The closest paper should make the new paper easier to understand, not trigger an anticipatory response letter.

## Comparison Reasoning

Use the following steps selectively when they help explain the paper’s actual economic contribution. They are reasoning cues for the prose, not mandatory stages, artifacts, or reasons to defer the assigned writing.

1. State the closest paper’s economic question and contribution fairly.
2. Define its economic object, unit, timing, and principal outcomes.
3. Identify the unresolved boundary or question.
4. State this paper’s economic object and conceptual relation:
   - narrower subset;
   - broader construct;
   - adjacent construct;
   - complement; or
   - direct competitor.
5. Derive the empirical implication of that relation.
6. Present incremental, exclusion, timing, or outcome evidence.
7. Use overlap statistics as descriptive support, not as the contribution.

A valid opening must positively state the neighboring economic object or the paper’s own economic question. Do not open with “the contribution does not rest on,” “the question is not whether,” or an equivalent negative contrast.

When a material fact about the nearest paper is missing, first check accessible primary literature and existing project sources. State only distinctions that the available evidence supports. Continue **writing the assigned manuscript passage** using the present paper’s established question, mechanism, and findings; a defensible narrower contribution can be stated without an unverified detailed comparison. If one precise comparator assertion remains unverifiable, omit or narrow that assertion, and flag the specific unresolved attribution outside the manuscript when it is consequential. **Do not replace requested prose with an outline, missing-evidence checklist, or new preliminary review task.** Never invent the neighbor’s research question, findings, or identifying assumptions.

## Relevant Comparison Dimensions

Examine the dimensions material to the claim, using accessible evidence. No comparison-matrix document is required unless expressly requested. Write the supported economic comparison in the paper itself. Relevant dimensions include:

- economic object;
- data and disclosure unit;
- sample and coverage;
- timing;
- measure or treatment;
- outcome;
- research design;
- conceptual overlap;
- unresolved economic question; and
- decisive incremental test.

## What Counts as Differentiation

Strong differentiation usually comes from:

- a distinct economic object;
- a different mechanism;
- evidence on an unresolved outcome or horizon;
- incremental explanatory or predictive content;
- a design that changes the available inference; or
- a boundary condition that changes theory or interpretation.

Differences in transcript provider, model version, segmentation, denominator, or matching rule may explain empirical divergence. They rarely carry the contribution by themselves.

Do not add a sentence listing implementation differences to the opening comparison paragraph merely to appear balanced. Include such a sentence later only when an observed empirical mismatch requires it.

## Fairness Test

The writing agent should check the actual manuscript against these questions:

1. Would the neighboring paper’s authors recognize their question and result?
2. Is the distinction stated before criticism or metric disagreement?
3. Does the new contribution survive removal of implementation differences?
4. Is the decisive comparison shown rather than asserted?

Resolve demonstrable positioning problems directly in the affected prose. These questions are editorial tests the writer can perform; they do not trigger a second agent. When a fresh scholarly reader is warranted, use the common [independent-reader.md](independent-reader.md) policy **after** the writer's own manuscript integration.
