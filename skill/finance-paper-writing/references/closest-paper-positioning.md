# Closest-Paper Positioning

Explain the **economic relationship** between the current paper and the nearest verified research. A fair comparison tells readers what the neighboring study establishes, the unresolved question, and which distinct inference the present paper's evidence supplies.

Consider the economic object, mechanism, relevant agents, outcome, timing, research design, and inferential difference. Distinctions in data provider, transcript segmentation, classifier version, sample-matching code, or processing workflow rarely establish a scholarly contribution by themselves. Overlap statistics may describe related constructs; they do not replace the economic argument.

Use accessible primary papers and project evidence to verify claims about neighboring research. If an important comparator fact is unavailable, **continue writing** the strongest defensible contribution based on the current study's established question and findings. Omit or narrow the unsupported comparator statement and briefly identify a consequential unresolved citation outside the manuscript if necessary. Do not invent the neighbor's results, return a missing-evidence checklist instead of prose, or launch a literature campaign unrelated to the assignment.

Keep the paragraph affirmative: explain both economic objects and the new question, then show the decisive comparison. Address an actual observed discrepancy or inferential limitation where it matters; avoid rehearsing hypothetical reviewer objections. The agent can assess fairness by asking whether the neighboring authors would recognize their stated result and whether the claimed distinction survives removing technical implementation differences. No separate evaluator or comparison matrix is required.
