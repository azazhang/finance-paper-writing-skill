# Finance Prose Rubric

Use this rubric for empirical corporate finance, asset pricing, banking, household finance, and adjacent accounting manuscripts. Judge prose by the reader’s inference, not by grammatical polish alone.

## Primary Standard

A finance reader should be able to recover:

1. the economic question;
2. why it matters;
3. what variation or setting the paper uses;
4. the main findings and their magnitude;
5. what the evidence can support;
6. how the paper differs from its closest literature; and
7. why the result changes what the reader knows.

If those answers are obscure, cleaner sentences do not solve the main prose problem.

## Failure Taxonomy

| Failure | Symptoms | Detection question | Repair |
|---|---|---|---|
| Research-workspace leakage | Local paths, file names, versions, pipelines, hashes, repair history | Would this sentence make sense without repository access? | Describe the source, sample, dates, filters, and construction rule |
| Method capture | The estimator, classifier, or data workflow becomes the paper’s subject | Would the economic question remain compelling if the method changed? | Restore economic object, mechanism, and evidence as the paragraph subjects |
| Defense-first architecture | Sections begin with what a test cannot do or why a comparison is unfair | Does the reader know the claim before meeting the defense? | Lead with the object, design, finding, and interpretation |
| Disclaimer cascade | Repeated “not causal,” “not X,” “cannot establish,” or “need not” statements | Is the limitation already encoded by the verb or stated elsewhere? | Keep one calibrated qualification in its primary home |
| Context compression | Undefined samples, timings, denominators, groups, or acronyms | Can a cold reader answer who, what, when, and relative to what? | Define at first use and use informative exhibit notes |
| Evidence inversion | Development diagnostics dominate evidence closest to the economic claim | Does prominence reflect inferential importance or development effort? | Reorder prose and exhibits by relevance to the paper’s claim |
| Competitor air defense | Rebuttal and overlap mechanics precede a fair account of the neighboring paper | Can the reader state both economic objects before the comparison statistics? | Explain the neighboring contribution, boundary, and incremental question first |
| Epistemic drift | Association becomes mechanism or prediction becomes causality | What design feature licenses the chosen verb? | Use the strongest verb supported by the design |
| Template accretion | Generic ethics, AI, authorship, funding, or limitations sections appear automatically | Is the section required or materially informative? | Apply a venue- and study-specific inclusion test |
| Literal repair | Flagged words disappear while the paragraph’s defensive job survives | Would this paragraph be written this way from a blank page? | Rebuild or remove the paragraph |

## Paragraph Test

For each substantive paragraph, identify:

- **job:** the one belief or transition the paragraph must earn;
- **evidence:** the result, design fact, or citation supporting it;
- **interpretation:** why that evidence matters economically;
- **boundary:** the one qualification required here, if any.

Delete sentences that perform no job. Split paragraphs performing unrelated jobs. Combine consecutive paragraphs that repeat the same claim with different throat-clearing.

## Section-Opening Test

Read only each heading and first sentence. Flag an opening that:

- begins with inability, ambiguity, a caveat, or a methodological defense;
- refers backward to project history;
- assumes an undefined construct or sample;
- announces the section rather than making an economic point; or
- foregrounds a secondary test before the primary result.

The opening need not always state a result, but it must orient the reader toward the section’s economic purpose.

## Finance Register

Prefer:

- concrete research subjects: firms, managers, investors, lenders, workers, markets;
- concrete data descriptions: source, coverage, sample, dates, filters;
- design-calibrated verbs: documents, is associated with, predicts, responds to, estimates;
- coefficient interpretations with units and economically meaningful magnitudes;
- ordinary author voice: “I” for a solo author’s coding or design judgment, otherwise natural impersonal prose; and
- short technical definitions followed by economic language.

Avoid:

- generic “researcher,” “principal investigator,” “teacher model,” or “agent” roles in reader-facing prose;
- software metaphors such as backbone, deployment, governance, or pipeline when describing the study;
- unexplained technical language in the abstract or introduction;
- “the appropriate test is” and similar referee-directed language;
- claims of novelty based mainly on file source, model version, or implementation detail; and
- uniform claim–caveat–disclaimer paragraphs that sound like legal briefing.

## Skimming Bundle

Review the following as one document:

1. title;
2. abstract;
3. first two introduction pages;
4. section headings;
5. main table and figure titles, panels, and notes;
6. conclusion.

Write what the paper appears to ask, find, contribute, and not establish. Any mismatch with the paper charter is a major prose issue.
