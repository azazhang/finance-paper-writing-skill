# Empirical Finance Section Jobs

Use section names appropriate to the paper. The jobs below are functional, not a mandatory template.

## Abstract

Answer in compact form:

1. What economic question is studied?
2. What new data, setting, or variation makes it answerable?
3. What are the strongest findings?
4. What is their economic interpretation?
5. What changes relative to the existing literature?

Prefer finance language over technical implementation detail. Include a material boundary only when omitting it would change the apparent contribution.

## Introduction

A common effective sequence is:

1. economic problem and importance;
2. missing observable, unresolved question, or empirical obstacle;
3. setting, measure, or design in plain language;
4. headline evidence and magnitude;
5. supporting timing, cross-sectional, mechanism, or validation evidence;
6. closest-paper differentiation through the economic question;
7. contributions and roadmap when useful.

Do not begin the empirical summary with what the design cannot identify. Do not make literature positioning a list of differences in data engineering.

## Economic Framework or Institutional Setting

Use this section to make predictions, interpretation, and relevant institutional detail clear. Do not manufacture formal hypotheses when the research is exploratory or the evidence does not support directional predictions.

## Data and Research Design

Describe:

- source and coverage;
- sample construction;
- unit and timing;
- variable construction;
- identifying or predictive variation;
- standard errors and fixed effects; and
- exclusions that matter economically.

Describe research objects rather than local storage or script order.

## Results

For each principal table or figure:

1. state the economic question;
2. explain the relevant specification only as needed;
3. report sign, magnitude, uncertainty, and units;
4. interpret economic meaning;
5. connect to the paper’s mechanism or boundary; and
6. qualify once where necessary.

Do not narrate every column mechanically.

## Closest Literature

Present the closest contribution fairly, define the unresolved economic boundary, and explain how the paper’s evidence addresses it. See `closest-paper-positioning.md`.

## Robustness, Alternative Explanations, and Validation

Organize around reader concerns, not development chronology. Lead each group with the concern and the evidence that resolves or narrows it. Do not make a catalog of tests whose relevance is left implicit.

## Conclusion

Restate:

- what the paper teaches;
- why it matters for finance;
- the supported boundary; and
- the most important open question when useful.

Do not introduce new results or repeat a long list of caveats.
