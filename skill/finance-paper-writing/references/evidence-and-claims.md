# Evidence and Claim Discipline

Build the paper from evidence that exists, but organize it around the economic question rather than the order in which analyses were completed.

## Evidence States

Distinguish three dimensions when selecting evidence; a new ledger is optional:

| Dimension | Values |
|---|---|
| Provenance | current, legacy, unknown |
| Verification | verified, provisional, failed, planned |
| Paper treatment | main, appendix, discussion, omit, active-development |

Quantitative claims must use the current, established evidence and the correct cohort and financial definitions. Do not reopen every legacy analysis: verify only a specific result needed for the assigned revision, using its existing outputs and code when sufficient.

## Inferential Status

| Status | Typical design | Allowed language |
|---|---|---|
| Descriptive | Counts, distributions, correlations without timing claim | documents, describes, tracks, varies with |
| Associational | Observational regressions | is associated with, covaries with, is related to |
| Predictive | Predictor observed before outcome with appropriate conditioning | predicts, forecasts, contains information about subsequent |
| Mechanism-consistent | Pattern aligned with a channel but not isolating it | is consistent with, is stronger when |
| Quasi-causal | Credible design-specific identifying variation | use design-specific treatment-response language |
| Causal | Identification supports causal interpretation | causes, raises, reduces, affects |

Do not repeat a generic noncausality disclaimer when the selected verbs and design discussion already set the claim boundary.

## Main Text Versus Appendix

Place evidence in the main text when it is required to believe:

- the economic question matters;
- the measure or treatment captures the stated object;
- the main result exists and is economically meaningful;
- the result differs from the closest alternative explanation or construct;
- the timing or design supports the claimed inference; or
- a salient concern would otherwise dominate a skimming reviewer’s judgment.

Use the Appendix for supporting comparisons and methodological detail that help assess the retained argument. Keep uninformative exploratory grids, development diagnostics, and the full attempted-test archive in the research record. Additional examples illustrate an established empirical pattern; they do not supply the main evidence.

Do not bury the strongest validation or differentiation evidence while displaying every internal diagnostic.

## Quantitative Sentence Check

Every quantitative sentence should identify or make recoverable:

- sample;
- unit;
- dependent variable;
- scale of the explanatory variable;
- estimate and uncertainty;
- timing;
- specification or exhibit; and
- inferential status.

The sentence need not repeat every field when the surrounding text and table make them unambiguous.

## Targeted claim-to-exhibit and citation consistency

When drafting or revising a quantitative claim, compare the **affected** wording directly with the current table, figure, or established result it cites. Reconcile the sample and observation unit, outcome, variable scale and denominator, coefficient or spread, sign, reference category, timing, uncertainty (standard error, confidence interval, or test), and exhibit number or panel. Distinguish percentage points from percentages, monthly from annual quantities, and within-sample contrasts from population statements. Compute only the simple transformations needed for the interpretation and check the arithmetic.

For changed literature claims, verify that each cited source actually supports the attributed economic question, result, design, and contribution; use the closest available primary source when feasible. A citation's presence does not establish support for the sentence. Preserve source uncertainty honestly if the specific attribution remains unresolved.

For whole-paper revision, align the headline economic claim and quantitative magnitudes across the abstract, introduction, main exhibits, and conclusion. For a paragraph or section edit, check its affected claims and surrounding transitions. Use existing research outputs and sources for these comparisons; do not restart estimation, collect new data, or create a separate evidence ledger unless that is the assigned work. Repair the actual prose or exhibit reference when a discrepancy is demonstrated.

## Null and Adverse Results

An informative null requires a credible design and sufficient precision to bear on an economic prediction. A wide interval is not evidence of no effect. Do not hide a material adverse result. Decide placement by relevance to the headline claim:

- main text if it narrows or materially qualifies the contribution;
- Appendix with a main-text pointer if it addresses a secondary horizon or outcome;
- active-development record if the design remains incomplete and no result is claimed.

Avoid speculative stories written solely to neutralize a null.
