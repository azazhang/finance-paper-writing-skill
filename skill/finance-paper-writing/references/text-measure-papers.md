# Writing About Text-Based Finance Measures

**Use only when the paper measures a construct from text, coding, classifiers, or language models.** Keep the main narrative about the **economic construct and financial question**.

Define what qualifies as the construct and its economic boundary, supply representative examples where relevant, and explain how passage-level labels aggregate to the firm, call, document, or other empirical unit. State the sampling frame and reference protocol for reported agreement statistics.

Distinguish the evidence's jobs:

- **Classifier fidelity:** agreement with a stated reference set of classifications; it does not independently validate the economic construct.
- **Construct validity:** evidence from appropriate examples, known-group patterns, convergent and discriminant measures, and outcome associations.
- **Aggregation and robustness:** whether the empirical-unit measure behaves as intended and economic conclusions survive plausible ambiguity, thresholds, or coding choices.
- **Human-coded reference:** a protocol-dependent comparator, not automatic ground truth. Deliberately difficult excerpts cannot establish population agreement without appropriate sampling.

Place the most informative **economic validity** evidence where it supports the paper's contribution. Technical classification details and auxiliary reliability diagnostics belong in the appendix when they are secondary. A material weakness must still be addressed at the level where it changes inference.

Use natural publication language: reference classifications, coded passages, empirical-unit measure, classifier fidelity, alternative definitions. Avoid front-loading software architecture, internal version identifiers, “teacher/student” metaphors, AI development history, or unsupported “gold standard” claims. Do not undertake another classifier or measurement exercise unless the author actually assigned one.
